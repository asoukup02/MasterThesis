\section{Compensation}
\nomenclature[A]{DFF}{Disturbance Feedforward}
\nomenclature[A]{NDI}{Nonlinear Dynamic Inversion}

While wind is modeled as an additional system state in the Kalman Filter implementation, it can also be considered as an external disturbance acting on the airship. Therefore, the wind estimation provided by the Kalman Filter can be utilized to compensate for the effect of wind disturbances.
Two compensation approaches are implemented and evaluated: a nonlinear Disturbance Feedforward (DFF) controller and a Nonlinear Dynamic Inversion (NDI) controller. Both approaches can be interpreted as an augmentation to the existing PID controller, adding an additional compensation term
\begin{equation}
	\bm{u} = \bm{u}_{\text{PID}} + \bm{u}_{\text{comp}},
	\label{eq:compensation_u}
\end{equation}


where $\bm{u}_{\text{PID}}$ is mainly responsible for setpoint tracking and stabilization, while $\bm{u}_{\text{comp}}$ uses model knowledge of the airship to counteract the wind-induced disturbances in case of DFF, or compensates for all acting forces and moments in case of the NDI.

To minimize the delay between wind disturbance estimation and compensation, the estimated disturbance is compensated directly at the force and moment level.Based on the estimated wind and the current airship state measurement, the required 
compensation force and moment vector 
\[
\bm{\mathcal{F}}_{\text{comp}} 
= 
\begin{pmatrix} \bm{F}_{\text{comp}} \\ \bm{M}_{\text{comp}} \end{pmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^6
\]
is calculated.
The computation of $\bm{\mathcal{F}}_{\text{comp}}$ depends on the compensation approach and is derived in the sections~\ref{sec:Disturbance_Feedforward} and \ref{sec:NDI}.

The total desired force and moment vector is obtained by combining the force and moment vector generated by the PID controller, $\bm{\mathcal{F}}_{\text{PID}}$, with the compensation vector, $\bm{\mathcal{F}}_{\text{comp}}$:
\begin{equation}
	\bm{\mathcal{F}} = \bm{\mathcal{F}}_{\text{PID}} + \bm{\mathcal{F}}_{\text{comp}}.
\end{equation}

This force and moment vector is subsequently converted into individual motor commands by the control allocation algorithm. Performing the compensation directly at the force and moment level enables computationally efficient execution, as the mapping from forces and moments to motor commands $\bm{u}$ is performed only once per time step. At the same time, the underlying concept introduced in Eq.~\eqref{eq:compensation_u} is preserved, allowing disturbances to be compensated with a delay of only one control cycle.

The original PID controller, without compensation, was designed with a strong emphasis on robustness against modeling uncertainties and wind disturbances. Since the compensation approaches explicitly account for a significant portion of these disturbances, the controller can theoretically be retuned to place greater emphasis on tracking performance. This results in a more aggressively tuned PID controller. In section \todo{ref} the effects are evaluated.

In the following sections, the two compensation approaches are described in detail, and the derivation of the compensation vector $\bm{\mathcal{F}}_{\text{comp}}$ is presented.


\subsection{Disturbance Feedforward}
\label{sec:Disturbance_Feedforward}
The Disturbance Feedforward compensates for the wind induced forces and moments only. This means $\bm{\mathcal{F}}_{comp}$ compensates for the additional forces and Moments created by the prevailing wind.
Looking at the dynamics one can see that wind affects our airship only via the aerodynamical forces, as they depend on the airspeed Va which is

\begin{equation}
	V_a = V_b - V_w
\end{equation}

\todo{add dynamics, what is Faero and Fprop?}
Fprop describes the propulsion force that is achieved by a command u. Faero is the aerodynamical force. In general Faero and Fprop describe nonlinear functions mapping u and Va to Forces. Fprop can be directly expressed by $\mathcal{F}$

we basically have everything expressed in forces.


The Disturbance Feedforward task is to find $u_{comp}$ s.t.
\begin{equation}
	F_{\text{aero}}(V_A) + F_{\text{prop}}(u + u_c)
	=
	F_{\text{aero}}(V_B) + F_{\text{prop}}(u)
\end{equation}

To further understand what this means lets look how this unfolds assuming a linear system.

\begin{align}
	F(V_A) + F_{\text{prop}}(u + u_c)
	&= F(V_B) - F(V_w) + F_{\text{prop}}(u) + F_{\text{prop}}(u_c) \\
	&= F(V_B) + F_{\text{prop}}(u), \\
	\Rightarrow\quad
	F_{\text{prop}}(u_c)
	&= F(V_w)
\end{align}

in this case F(vw) simply describes the additional forces induced by wind. 
For nonlinear systems its more complicated.


Tuning: The PID controller is retuned on the undisturbed model, as the compensation should eliminate those, allowing for a faster PID control. 
In \todo{ref} the performance of the retuned PID controller is examined and compared to the control structure with only the PID.
It shows that the retuned PID is far too aggressive and not able to achieve good performance for all flight envelopes, although performing better on some. The reason for this is that although theoretically the Compensation should compensate for the wind, it is not able to fully cancel it due to uncertainties in the wind estimation and state measurements. Hence a robust controller is desired anyways. A combination of the robust PID with the compensation is beneficial.
