\section{Compensation}
Two approaches are implemented and compared to compensate for the effect of wind. A nonlinear Disturbance Feedforward and NDI.
The basic idea of either approach is to extend the current PID control with an compensation term as

\begin{equation}
	u = u_{PID} + u_{comp}
\end{equation}

to achieve a partial or full compensation of the wind forces or total forces with $u_{comp}$ and a setpoint tracking behaviour via $u_{PID}$ for the remaining dynamics.

To achieve the fastest possible compensation we compute the compensation directly at an Force level. Adding them up with the desired forces from the PID yields the total desired forces. The allocator turns these desired forces into an control command for the individual motors.

\subsection{Disturbance Feedforward}
The Disturbance Feedforward compensates for the wind induced forces and moments. This means $u_{comp}$ compensates for the additional forces and Moments created by the prevailing wind.
Looking at the dynamics one can see that wind affects our airship only via the aerodynamical forces, as they depend on the airspeed Va which is

\begin{equation}
	V_a = V_b - V_w
\end{equation}

The Disturbance Feedforward task is to find $u_{comp}$ s.t.
\begin{equation}
	F_{\text{aero}}(V_A) + F_{\text{prop}}(u + u_c)
	=
	F_{\text{aero}}(V_B) + F_{\text{prop}}(u)
\end{equation}

To further understand what this means lets look how this unfolds assuming a linear system.

\begin{align}
	F(V_A) + F_{\text{prop}}(u + u_c)
	&= F(V_B) - F(V_w) + F_{\text{prop}}(u) + F_{\text{prop}}(u_c) \\
	&= F(V_B) + F_{\text{prop}}(u), \\
	\Rightarrow\quad
	F_{\text{prop}}(u_c)
	&= F(V_w)
\end{align}

in this case F(vw) simply describes the additional forces induced by wind. 
For nonlinear systems its more complicated.
